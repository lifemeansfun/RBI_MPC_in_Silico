## Findings verified in primary sources

1. **Real-time vintage and endpoint information are separate requirements.** Orphanides and van Norden's [FEDS 1999-38 working paper](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/1999/199938/199938pap.pdf), section 2.3 and Appendix, distinguishes real-time estimates using each contemporary vintage from quasi-real estimates using truncated final data. Their US evidence shows substantial, persistent revisions and prominent endpoint uncertainty. This warrants separate provenance and filter-sensitivity checks; it does not quantify India's uncertainty. Inspect `text/ovn_1999.txt`, PDF pages 6–9 and 30–31.

2. **Prefix-only HP has a precise construction, with continuing limitations.** Hamilton's [author working paper revised 2017-05-13](https://econweb.ucsd.edu/~jhamilto/hp.pdf), section 3.2, describes retaining each sample endpoint from successively truncated HP fits. One-sided construction removes dependence on unknown future realizations, while the trend and cycle can retain filter-induced dynamics. Sections 3–4 criticize conventional smoothing and propose a regression using four lagged observations with an eight-quarter horizon. Inspect `text/hamilton_2017.txt`, PDF pages 9–11 and 13–16. The downloaded working-paper version must not be described as the archived 2018 journal PDF.

3. **Low revision size is insufficient to identify economic slack.** Gruen, Robinson and Stone's [RBA RDP 2002-06](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/rdp/2002/pdf/rdp2002-06.pdf), sections 2–3, distinguishes recorded historical assessments from reconstructed econometric ones. It illustrates that HP estimates can have relatively small mean revisions yet differ substantially from estimates informed by the inflation relationship. Inspect `text/gruen_robinson_stone_2002.txt`, PDF pages 7–8 and 24–27.

4. **An alternative filter carries additional identifying assumptions.** Kamber, Morley and Wong's [BIS Working Paper 584](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-584-intuitive-and-reliable-estimates-output-gap-beveridge-nelson-filter.pdf) imposes a lower trend-shock signal-to-noise ratio in a Beveridge–Nelson decomposition. Their US results support considering estimator dependence, not importing their reliability result to a short Indian vintage history. Section 3.1 discusses weak identification even in considerably larger simulated samples. Inspect `text/kamber_morley_wong_2016_pdf.txt`, PDF pages 4, 14–17 and 19–22. The legacy `work584.pdf` URL now returns a landing page; that response is preserved separately.

5. **The literature does not establish universal hopelessness either.** Edge and Rudd's [FEDS 2012-86](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2012/201286/201286pap.pdf), sections III/V, finds improved revision properties for later Federal Reserve staff assessments, without corresponding general improvement in purely statistical alternatives. Champagne, Poulin-Bellisle and Sekkel's [Bank of Canada paper 2016-28](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/swp2016-28.pdf) examines contemporary staff assessments as well. These institutional estimates combine more information and judgement than the proposed GDP-only proxy. Neither source establishes its reliability. Inspect the corresponding `edge_rudd_2012.txt` and `champagne_poulin_sekkel_2016.txt`.

6. **A seasonal-adjustment minimum is not an output-gap reliability minimum.** The [IMF QNA manual, 2017 edition](https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/qna/pdf/2017/QNAManual2017text.pdf), paragraphs 7.114–7.117 and footnote 70, recommends at least 20 quarterly observations for QNA seasonal adjustment, with satisfactory stability before publication. The software's smaller runnable minimum is not the recommendation. Paragraphs 7.65–7.75 explain endpoint revisions and update strategies. Inspect `text/imf_qna_2017.txt`, PDF pages 159–162 and 173. **Metadata correction:** this downloaded manual has a 2018 imprint; primary eLibrary metadata gives print publication date 2018-11-06. The edition label is not evidence of 2017 publication. The immutable acquisition ledger's initial 2017 date interpretation is superseded by the verified bibliography.

7. **Seven years is a useful conservative design boundary, not a theorem.** [Eurostat ESS 2009 guidelines](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/documents/3859598/5910549/KS-RA-09-006-EN.PDF.pdf/0a9893a6-3e69-45c4-8328-6fc5a3497c13?t=1414781563000), section 5.1, flags instability for 3–7-year series and more frequent parameter review below seven years. [ESS 2024 guidelines](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/documents/3859598/19355229/KS-GQ-24-012-EN-N.pdf), section 8.3, makes a related short-series distinction. Neither provides a validated HP sample-size threshold. Inspect `text/eurostat_2009.txt`, PDF page 30, and `text/eurostat_2024.txt`, PDF pages 35–36.

8. **Large nonrepeating shocks should remain economic movements.** ESS 2024 section 8.2 explains why crises should not become seasonal effects and why endpoint outlier classification is difficult. This supports an explicit warning that a simple quarterly-dummy regression can absorb some COVID movements into its seasonal coefficients. It does not prescribe the pandemic sensitivity below. The [Census X-13 manual, version 1.1 dated 2017-01-18](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/working-papers/2017/adrm/docx13ashtml.pdf) documents fixed seasonal regressors and revision/sliding-span diagnostics. Inspect `text/census_x13_2017.txt`, PDF page 40 and printed pages 55–58. Using a seasonal-dummy OLS model is a simplification, not implementation of X-13's complete procedure.

## Proposed protocol to freeze before computing gaps

Let C be the declared information cutoff, and T the latest reference quarter for which genuine published GDP is documented as available by C. Use only a homogeneous, contiguous real-GDP level history available at C, with recorded source hashes, publication/vintage evidence and units/base year. A later publication containing older quarters is still a later vintage. Do not add its historical values to earlier cutoffs. Preserve unknown publication availability and missing quarters; do not interpolate dates, levels, annual GDP into quarters, or future backcasts. Prefer a complete contemporaneous vintage. If multiple previously published sources are combined, freeze their precedence and revision-consistency rules separately and retain each cell's source identity.

**Primary eligibility:** at least 28 consecutive finite, strictly positive quarterly GDP levels, with seven observations of every quarter. This is an explicit conservative study choice informed by seasonal-adjustment guidance. It is not a numerical requirement for solving HP, a guarantee of potential-output identification, or proof of stable seasonal effects. Use all eligible history in the expanding prefix. Below this threshold, return a missing primary gap with the reason; do not silently lower the threshold or switch vintage.

For each cutoff, set x_j = log(GDP_j). Fit, on that cutoff's available prefix only, x_j = a + b j + s_q(j) + e_j with sum_q s_q = 0. Define z_j = x_j − estimated s_q(j). The linear trend identifies seasonal coefficients; do not call its estimate structural potential output. The seasonal model assumes constant proportional quarterly effects and does not adjust moving holidays or working-day changes. Algorithm settings are frozen while coefficients are estimated afresh using each cutoff's permissible information. Store all coefficients and diagnostics.

Fit HP to z_1,…,z_n with λ=1600 by minimizing sum_j(z_j−τ_j)^2 + λ sum_{j=3}^n(τ_j−2τ_{j−1}+τ_{j−2})^2. Retain only endpoint τ_n for that cutoff. Append no forecasts or realized future quarters. Define the primary proxy as 100(z_n−τ_n), explicitly in **log percentage points**. If exact percentage deviation 100(exp(z_n−τ_n)−1) is desired, freeze it as a separate reported transformation rather than interchange units, especially for large contractions. Record T, C, publication evidence, GDP age, history start/end, seasonal coefficients, λ, input/code/config hashes and the immutable endpoint output. This is slack relative to a statistical trend; no structural capacity, welfare or real-policy causal interpretation follows.

**Predeclared sensitivities:** report λ=400 and λ=6400 under identical inputs and seasonal treatment; a 40-quarter minimum changes eligibility only on the same expanding history and should be called a coverage/warm-up sensitivity. A trailing-40-quarter estimator changes the fit and requires at least 40 genuinely available quarters. Compare methods on their common eligible observations, and disclose uncovered cutoffs. A separate pandemic sensitivity may estimate seasonal effects from only as-of-available quarters ending no later than calendar 2019Q4, requiring 28 such quarters, and then apply those effects to the entire available series before HP. This is a substantive assumption to freeze before gap outputs, not an official recommended repair. Do not alter shocks, seasonality or λ after inspecting prediction results.

A deterministic linear-trend gap or a genuinely prefix-fitted Hamilton regression can be additional frozen alternatives. They identify different statistical objects; with 28 quarters, the eight-quarter/four-lag Hamilton regression has only 17 usable response rows and five fitted coefficients. That arithmetic is not evidence of adequate estimation. No inspected primary source gives a universal validated short-history minimum for HP, Hamilton or BN.

## Verification scope and uncertainty disclosure

Implementation checks should test future-data exclusion, vintage/cutoff rejection, finite positive levels, contiguous quarters, reproducible endpoints, constant-unit rescaling invariance, zero-sum seasonal coefficients and immutable input/config identities. These are engineering tests only; they do not validate measured slack.

For scientific diagnostics, retain every estimator result, sign disagreement and dispersion; show effects of λ, history window and seasonal treatment without choosing a winner from evaluation scores. If adequate real-time vintages exist, separately measure revision effects on a fixed reference quarter and changes when later quarters extend the filter. Truncated final data belong in a clearly labeled pseudo-real-time diagnostic, never the primary dated information set. An estimator range is not a calibrated confidence interval. Short-history failures and missing vintage evidence remain unresolved observations, not zeros.

The source archive verifies the cited methodological propositions and supports this transparent proxy protocol. It does not establish that the proposed proxy has acceptable error, that 28 quarters are empirically sufficient for India, or that it improves any forecasting comparator.
