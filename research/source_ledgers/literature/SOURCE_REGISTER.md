## Kazinnik and Sinclair: FOMC In Silico

The [SSRN author record](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5424097) identifies a revision dated 12 July 2026, while retaining an original writing date of 31 August 2025. Its abstract reports a 218-meeting backtest through June 2026, 8.3 bps rate MAE and 91% hike/cut/hold accuracy. Those are author-reported results on a different committee and sample, not independently verified benchmarks for this project. The revised abstract describes deliberation as building consensus rather than improving forecasting.

## Riboni and Ruge-Murcia (2010)

[Monetary policy by committee: Consensus, chairman dominance, or simple majority?](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/monetary-policy-by-committee-consensus-chairman-dominance-or-simp/), *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 125(1), 363-416. DOI: [10.1162/qjec.2010.125.1.363](https://doi.org/10.1162/qjec.2010.125.1.363).

The authors compare alternative committee decision models and estimate them against policy decisions. This provides a reason to examine voting and agenda assumptions rather than treating a chosen aggregation formula as a derived equilibrium. Applying a model's name does not transfer that paper's theoretical identification or empirical fit to another implementation.

Discovery used a fetched [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/monetary-policy-by-committee-consensus-chairman-riboni-ruge-murcia/b5a2171ac973513a8c0b641211b48c91/?utm_source=chatgpt); publication metadata was checked against the author's institutional record above. Citation counts are provider-dependent and are not evidence of methodological correctness.

## Baerg and Lowe: textual preferences

[A textual Taylor rule: estimating central bank preferences combining topic and scaling methods](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-science-research-and-methods/article/abs/textual-taylor-rule-estimating-central-bank-preferences-combining-topic-and-scaling-methods/E1CAE25BBA2864109ACF25D599EDD083), *Political Science Research and Methods*, 8(1), January 2020, 106-122; published online 18 September 2018. DOI: [10.1017/psrm.2018.31](https://doi.org/10.1017/psrm.2018.31).

This work estimates preference measures from text through topic and scaling methods without defining them directly by voting outcomes. It is relevant to constructing and validating persona measures separately from the outcomes used to evaluate those personas. It does not validate an arbitrary dictionary score or mixture weight in this project.

The fetched [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/a-textual-taylor-rule-estimating-central-bank-preferences-baerg-lowe/947ced3163f25134910e0b53a3d2df72/?utm_source=chatgpt) gave 2015 as its year; the primary publisher distinguishes the 2018 online date from the 2020 issue. Use the primary publication metadata in bibliographies.
