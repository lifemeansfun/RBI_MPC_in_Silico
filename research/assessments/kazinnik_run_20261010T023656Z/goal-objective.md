2. FREEZE ONE MEANINGFUL INCREMENTAL QUESTION

Choose and justify one primary scientific question using calibration/development evidence before new evaluation. Explain what it adds beyond geography and additional engineering.

Institutional rules, information visibility or persona/deliberation mechanisms may motivate the question, but their importance and identification must be argued.

Treat these outcomes separately:

• Forecasting: exact rate, basis-point error, hike/cut/hold and committee stance.
• Dissent: exact-rate disagreement and formal stance dissent, separately from literal ballots, reservations and guidance disagreement.
• Consensus: predeclared ballot agreement and preference dispersion across rounds. Distinguish the proposed mechanism from copying the chair, aggregation or coercive instructions.

Deliberation need not improve forecasts to yield an informative result. More consensus does not establish better policy or real-world institutional benefit. Positive, null and negative findings are acceptable. An imprecise estimate is not proof of no effect or equivalence.

Freeze the primary claim, estimand, endpoints, cohort/eligibility, missing-history/label policy, source vintages and cutoffs, personas, models, prompts, comparators, resource matching, sampling, failure rules and statistical analysis.
