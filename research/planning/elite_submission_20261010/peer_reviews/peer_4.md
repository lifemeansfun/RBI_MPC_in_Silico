**B is strongest.** It connects verified implementation defects to the precise estimand, distinguishes generation uncertainty from historical and future-meeting uncertainty, and requires a meaningful effect threshold before replication planning. Those distinctions follow the evidence packet and prevent additional replicates from masquerading as additional temporal evidence.

**C has the most consequential scope blind spot:** its universal persona-and-roster integration gate could delay a defensible, explicitly artificial simulator experiment. Counterexample: randomizing information supplied to one fixed synthetic Governor can identify a conditional model response even with an oracle roster and unvalidated personas. Authenticity becomes necessary for forecasting or human-mechanism claims. This is a scope concern, not justification for relaxing provenance or overstating realism.

**All five lack an explicit rival-hypothesis prediction table.** Even a clean ballot-only effect could reflect generic numerical anchoring rather than sensitivity to binding decisions. After funding and identification gates pass, the cheapest discriminating observation would reuse one authentic development prefix and compare Governor responses to identical tuples described as binding votes versus nonbinding suggestions, alongside an undisclosed condition. Three branch calls could expose whether the claimed binding status matters; one prefix would remain a diagnostic, not confirmatory evidence.
